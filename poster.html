Reduced-lead electrocardiography / Patient-level selective prediction

When can two-lead ECG be trusted?

A diagnosis-specific reliability map connecting model performance to a decision to automate or abstain.

01 The clinical gap

Portable ECG could expand access where full 12-lead acquisition is difficult. But strong average accuracy does not establish which diagnostic decisions can be automated.

Research question

Can leads I + II rule out atrial fibrillation or flutter while keeping error among accepted negatives below 2%?

03 From probability to an action

Require evidence before accepting a call.

Risk = P(disease present | accepted negative)

Primary target: AFIB-or-AFLT. Accept a rule-out only if the validation upper error bound is ≤ 2%; otherwise escalate.

Select one outcome-blind ECG per patient. Split fold 9 into independent groups for Platt calibration, two-lead threshold selection and referred-case rescue selection.

Statistical safeguard: exact one-sided binomial bounds with Bonferroni correction over 101 thresholds and four stage/direction families [3]. Freeze policies before test export.

At 95% confidence, the corrected 2% bound needs ≥446 error-free accepted patients.

MAIN FINDINGUseful discrimination is not enough: automation must also meet the risk limit.
06 Research contribution

A reliability map with explicit abstention.

Method: connect diagnosis-specific model comparisons to a patient-level decision policy with independent calibration and frozen risk limits [2].

Value: report automation and missed cases together. Retain the 2% result; relaxed limits are exploratory, not a revised clinical target.

Low accepted-call error can still conceal substantial disease misses.

07 Limits & next experiments

Feasibility evidence, not clinical validation.

Uncertainty: one seed; previously inspected test data; sparse subgroups. Masked hospital ECGs do not reproduce wearables.

Next: complete seeds 43/44, then freeze the policy for an untouched external cohort and actual two-lead device recordings.

Risk limits require clinical justification and independent validation.
