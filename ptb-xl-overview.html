1. Dataset overview

PTB-XL is the largest publicly available 12-lead clinical ECG dataset. It was released by Wagner et al. (2020) and is hosted on PhysioNet under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. Each record is a conventional 10-second, 12-lead resting ECG acquired with commercial devices, accompanied by human- and machine-generated reports and SCP-ECG-coded labels. It is the standard benchmark for deep-learning ECG research.

What makes it suitable for the proposed research

  • True 12-lead simultaneous recordings → reduced-lead subsets can be created by simple lead masking without re-acquisition.
  • SCP-ECG labels map cleanly to the diagnostic superclasses used in the paper: MI (myocardial infarction), STTC (ST/T changes), CD (conduction disturbance), HYP (hypertrophy), NORM. AFIB and AFLT codes support the atrial-fibrillation/flutter target.
  • Pre-built strat_fold column provides patient-level folds 1–10, matching the train(1–8)/val(9)/test(10) protocol in the abstract.
  • Rich signal-quality columns (baseline_drift, static_noise, burst_noise, electrodes_problems, extra_beats, pacemaker) enable the abstract's noise/artifact stress-test of abstention.
  • age and sex are present for subgroup reliability analysis.
  • Open licence and broad adoption → reproducible, comparable results.

WFDB signal files

Each ECG is stored as a pair under records100/00000/00001_lr.dat + 00001_lr.hea (and the _hr equivalent at 500 Hz). The .hea header declares 12 signals, the sampling rate, gain, and per-lead baseline/ADC info. Example header:

00001_lr 12 100 1000
00001_lr.dat 16 1000.0(0)/mV 16 0 -119 1508 0 I
00001_lr.dat 16 1000.0(0)/mV 16 0 -55  723  0 II
... (III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1, V2, V3, V4, V5, V6)

Signals are 16-bit signed integers, gain 1000.0 (nV per bit), in mV. Each .dat holds 12 channels × 1000 samples (100 Hz × 10 s) or 5000 samples at 500 Hz.

3. Signals & leads

Every recording contains the full standard 12-lead ECG in this canonical order (as listed in the .hea file):

V1
V2

V4

Green = limb leads (I–aVF); blue = precordial leads (V1–V6). Lead indices are 0-based in the .dat array.

  • Limb leads (indices 0–5): I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF. aVR/aVL/aVF are derived from I and II by the device.
  • Precordial leads (indices 6–11): V1–V6. Require chest electrodes; typically the most informative for MI/ST-T and the first to be dropped in portable configs.
  • All leads are simultaneous (no multiplexing), so masking preserves temporal alignment — essential for the lead-masking experiments.

Important: aVR, aVL, aVF are linear combinations of I and II (aVR = −(I+II)/2, aVL = I−II/2, aVF = I/2+II). When retaining only I and II, the augmented leads carry no independent information; experiments should decide whether to recompute them or drop them.

Rhythm codes (for AF/AFL target)

Atrial fibrillation/flutter are SCP codes (not a separate superclass) and must be extracted directly from scp_codes:

  • AFIB — atrial fibrillation: 1,514 records
  • AFLT — atrial flutter: ~150 records
  • Also relevant: SBRAD (sinus bradycardia), STACH (sinus tachycardia), SARRH (sinus arrhythmia), PVC (1,143), SR (sinus rhythm, 16,748 — near-ubiquitous, usually dropped as a trivial label).
AFib/AFL is sparse. AFLT especially (<1% prevalence). Selective prediction here should account for class imbalance — consider per-class conformal thresholds and report per-class coverage, not just macro-averaged metrics.

7. Signal-quality metadata (for abstention stress-tests)

Six columns in ptbxl_database.csv describe recording artefacts. The abstract proposes to “stress-test abstention under noise and artifact” — these columns are the mechanism.

ColumnFlagged recordsFormatInterpretation
baseline_drift1,598 (7.3%)Lead list, e.g. ' , V6'Low-frequency wander; affects amplitude/ST measurements.
static_noise3,260 (14.9%)Lead listContinuous noise; degrades waveform morphology.
burst_noise613 (2.8%)Lead list or 'alles'Transient spikes; segment-level corruption.
electrodes_problems30 (0.14%)Lead nameElectrode misplacement/contact; very sparse.
extra_beats1,949 (8.7%)Code: 1ES/2ES/SVES/VESEctopic beats; affects rhythm labels.
pacemaker291 (1.3%)Free textPaced rhythm; typically exclude or analyse separately.

Suggested protocol: train on all validated records; at evaluation, partition the test fold into clean vs. noisy subsets (any of the flags non-empty) and compare accuracy–coverage curves. A well-calibrated selective model should abstain more often on noisy recordings.

11. Usage notes & gotchas

  • Parse scp_codes safely: it is a Python dict literal stored as a string. Use ast.literal_eval(row['scp_codes']), never eval.
  • Multi-label: a record can carry several SCP codes; superclass aggregation via scp_statements.csv yields a set of classes per record (one-hot multi-label, not softmax).
  • SR (sinus rhythm) is near-ubiquitous (16,748 records) and is a rhythm modifier, not a diagnostic superclass — typically dropped before training.
  • Age 300 is a censoring cap, not a real age. Treat ages ≥90 as a censored bucket in subgroup analysis. A common heuristic: clip to 90 or use age bands.
  • Lead order is fixed in every .hea: I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1, V2, V3, V4, V5, V6. Do not assume alphabetical order.
  • Sampling rate: use 100 Hz (filename_lr) for faster experimentation, 500 Hz (filename_hr) for final/higher-fidelity models. Both refer to the same physical recording.
  • Filter by validated_by_human == True (16,056 records) for the primary analysis; the 5,743 machine-only records are noisier labels.
  • Pacemaker records (291) should generally be excluded or analysed separately — paced confounds morphology-based labels.
  • Height/weight are sparse (32%/43%) — not reliable for BMI-based stratification across the full cohort.
  • v1.0.3 changelog lists duplicate removal and consensus-label fixes from earlier versions; always use 1.0.3 (this version).
  • Loading requires wfdb, pandas, numpy. The bundled example_physionet.py shows the canonical loading and split pattern.
